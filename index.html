PEOPLE · MUSIC · LIVING HERITAGE

The story behind the sound.

In Zimbabwe, mbira connects music with culture, history, spirituality and identity. Explore the instruments that inspired this digital space.

A sound rooted in community

Metal keys are mounted on a wooden soundboard and plucked to create the mbira’s ringing tones. A calabash resonator, often called a deze, can surround the instrument to amplify its sound. Rattles add a buzzing texture.

Mbira traditions in Zimbabwe are particularly associated with Shona communities, while other communities also make and play the instrument. Music, craftsmanship and knowledge are passed between generations.

Experiencing living heritage

A performance, a conversation with an instrument maker or a community-led workshop can offer visitors a deeper understanding of Zimbabwe. These encounters connect the music to the people and places that sustain it.

The supplied background document emphasises keeping communities at the centre of safeguarding mbira traditions, so that cultural tourism respects the music’s meaning and context.
